7.1 Speed of Wave Motion

The speed of wave motion depends on the elastic properties and inertial properties of the medium through which the wave propagates.

Fundamental Relationship of Wave Velocity
The velocity $v$ of a mechanical wave in any medium is given by: $$v = \sqrt{\frac{\text{Elastic Property}}{\text{Inertial Property}}} = \sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho}}$$ where $E$ is the elastic modulus ($\text{Pa}$ or $\text{N/m}^2$) and $\rho$ is the mass density ($\text{kg/m}^3$).

Physical Interpretation

  • Elasticity ($v \propto \sqrt{E}$): Greater elasticity provides a stronger restoring force, allowing mechanical disturbances to propagate faster.
  • Density ($v \propto 1/\sqrt{\rho}$): Greater density represents higher particle inertia, which resists acceleration and reduces propagation speed.

Elastic Moduli Across Media

Elastic Modulus Definition Formula Applicable Media & Wave Type
Young's Modulus ($Y$) Resistance to elongation/compression $Y = \frac{F/A}{\Delta L/L}$ Solids (Longitudinal waves in rods)
Bulk Modulus ($K$) Resistance to uniform volume change $K = -V \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V}$ Fluids & Solids (Volume compression)
Rigidity Modulus ($\eta$) Resistance to shear deformation $\eta = \frac{F/A}{\theta}$ Solids only (Transverse waves)
Adiabatic Bulk Modulus ($K_{\text{ad}}$) Volume elasticity under adiabatic state $K_{\text{ad}} = \gamma P$ Gases (Sound waves)

General Wave Velocity Expressions

$$\text{Transverse Waves in Strings/Rods: } v = \sqrt{\frac{\eta}{\rho}} \quad \text{or} \quad v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$$ $$\text{Longitudinal Waves in Solid Rods: } v = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}}$$ $$\text{Longitudinal Waves in Liquids: } v = \sqrt{\frac{K}{\rho}}$$ $$\text{Longitudinal Waves in Gases: } v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}}$$

7.2 Velocity of Sound in Solids and Liquids

Derivation for Solid Rods

Assume velocity $v$ depends on Young's Modulus $Y$ and density $\rho$ via power law:

$$v = k Y^a \rho^b$$

Dimensional analysis:

$$[v] = [L T^{-1}], \quad [Y] = [M L^{-1} T^{-2}], \quad [\rho] = [M L^{-3}]$$ $$[L T^{-1}] = [M L^{-1} T^{-2}]^a \cdot [M L^{-3}]^b = [M^{a+b} L^{-a-3b} T^{-2a}]$$

Equating exponents:

$$M: a + b = 0 \implies b = -a$$ $$T: -2a = -1 \implies a = 1/2 \implies b = -1/2$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}}$$

Velocity in Liquids

Liquids cannot sustain static shear stress, so sound propagates purely as longitudinal waves governed by Bulk Modulus ($K$):

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{K}{\rho}}$$

7.3 Velocity of Sound in Gases

1. Newton's Formula (Isothermal Model)

Newton assumed heat generated during compression rapidly dissipates ($T = \text{const}$). For isothermal expansion/compression:

$$P V = \text{constant} \implies P dV + V dP = 0 \implies P = -\frac{dP}{dV/V} = K_{\text{iso}}$$
$$v_{\text{Newton}} = \sqrt{\frac{P}{\rho}}$$

At STP, $v_{\text{Newton}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.013 \times 10^5}{1.293}} \approx 280\text{ m/s}$. Experimental value is $332\text{ m/s}$ ($16\%$ error).

2. Laplace's Correction (Adiabatic Model)

Laplace corrected this by noting that oscillations occur rapidly with zero time for heat exchange ($Q = 0$). For adiabatic process:

$$P V^\gamma = \text{constant} \implies V^\gamma dP + P \gamma V^{\gamma-1} dV = 0 \implies \gamma P = -\frac{dP}{dV/V} = K_{\text{ad}}$$
$$v_{\text{Laplace}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}}$$

For air ($\gamma = 1.40$), $v_{\text{Laplace}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.40 \times 101300}{1.293}} \approx 331.1\text{ m/s}$ (matches experiment).

7.4 Solved Examples & Self-Study Questions

Example 7.1: Temperature Scaling in Gas

Problem: At what temperature will the speed of sound in oxygen ($\text{O}_2, M=32\text{ g/mol}, \gamma=1.40$) equal that in helium ($\text{He}, M=4\text{ g/mol}, \gamma=1.67$) at $0^\circ\text{C}$?

Solution:

$$\sqrt{\frac{1.40 \cdot R \cdot T_{\text{O}_2}}{32 \times 10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.67 \cdot R \cdot 273}{4 \times 10^{-3}}}$$ $$\frac{1.40 \cdot T_{\text{O}_2}}{32} = \frac{1.67 \cdot 273}{4} \implies T_{\text{O}_2} \approx 2605.2\text{ K} = 2332.2^\circ\text{C}$$
Q1: Why was Laplace's correction necessary for Newton's sound velocity equation?

Newton assumed sound propagation was isothermal ($K = P$), which gave $280\text{ m/s}$ at STP. Sound oscillations happen too fast for heat transfer, making it an adiabatic process ($K = \gamma P$). Laplace's formula gives $331.1\text{ m/s}$, matching experimental findings.
