Group A: Multiple Choice Questions [11 × 1 = 11]
1 If a gymnast on a rotating stool with his arms outstretched suddenly lowers his arms,
a) the moment of inertia decreases
b) the angular momentum increases
c) the angular velocity decreases
d) the angular velocity remains constant
Answer: a) the moment of inertia decreases

Explanation: When the gymnast lowers his arms, the mass distribution moves closer to the axis of rotation. This reduces the moment of inertia (I = Σmr²). Since no external torque acts, angular momentum L = Iω is conserved. As I decreases, ω increases.

2 The motion of a particle executing simple harmonic motion is given by x = 0.01 sin 100π(t + 0.05), where x is in meters and t in second. The time period in second is
a) 0.001
b) 0.02
c) 0.1
d) 0.2
Answer: b) 0.02

Solution: Comparing with x = A sin(ωt + φ), we get ω = 100π rad/s.

T = 2π/ω = 2π/(100π) = 2/100 = 0.02 s
3 What fraction of a wooden raft of density 0.8 g/cc will be outside the sea water of density 1.2 g/cc?
a) 2/3
b) 1/2
c) 2/5
d) 1/3
Answer: d) 1/3

Solution: By law of floatation:

ρraft × V × g = ρwater × Vsub × g
Vsub/V = ρraft/ρwater = 0.8/1.2 = 2/3

Fraction outside = 1 − 2/3 = 1/3

4 In figure, how much work is done by the system in process AB?
a) 4.5 × 10³ J
b) 6.0 × 10³ J
c) 6.9 × 10³ J
d) 7.8 × 10³ J
Answer: c) 6.9 × 10³ J

Solution: Work done = Area under P-V curve (trapezium AB)

PA = 2.6 × 10&sup6; N/m², PB = 2.0 × 10&sup6; N/m²
VA = 1.0 × 10³³ m³, VB = 4.0 × 10³³ m³

W = ½(PA + PB) × (VB − VA)
W = ½(2.6 + 2.0) × 10&sup6; × (4.0 − 1.0) × 10³³
W = 6.9 × 10³ J
5 The diagram shows the pressure volume relationship for a fixed mass of an ideal gas that undergoes a cycle XYZ. In which part(s) of the cycle is external work done on the gas?
a) Y → Z only
b) Y → Z and Z → X only
c) X → Y and Z → X only
d) X → Y only
Answer: d) X → Y only

Explanation: External work is done ON the gas when the gas is compressed (volume decreases).

  • X → Y: Volume decreases (compression) → Work done on gas ✓
  • Y → Z: Volume constant (isochoric) → No work done
  • Z → X: Volume increases (expansion) → Work done by gas
6 The coefficient of performance of a refrigerator working between the ice point (0°C) and room temperature 27°C is
a) 10
b) 15
c) 20
d) 25
Answer: a) 10
Tcold = 0°C = 273 K
Thot = 27°C = 300 K

COP = Tcold / (Thot − Tcold)
COP = 273 / (300 − 273) = 273 / 27 ≈ 10
7 A diffraction pattern is obtained using a beam of red light. What will be the effect on the diffraction pattern if the red light is replaced by white light?
a) All bright fringes become white.
b) All bright fringes except central one, becomes white.
c) All bright fringes become colorful.
d) All bright fringes except central one, becomes colorful.
Answer: d) All bright fringes except central one, becomes colorful.

The central maximum contains all wavelengths superimposed, so it appears white. Higher-order fringes spread into spectra because fringe position y = nλD/d depends on wavelength. Each order becomes a rainbow (colorful), with red farthest from center and violet closest.

8 The sound levels of two waves of same frequency in a given medium are 100 dB and 20 dB respectively. How many times is the intensity I1 greater than I2?
a) 5
b) 8
c) 105
d) 108
Answer: d) 108
β1 − β2 = 10 log(I1/I2)
100 − 20 = 10 log(I1/I2)
80 = 10 log(I1/I2)
log(I1/I2) = 8
I1/I2 = 108
9 If the emf of a thermocouple, one junction of which is kept at 0°C, is given by E = aθ + ½bθ², where a and b are constants, then the neutral temperature will be
a) −a/b
b) a/b
c) −2a/b
d) 2a/b
Answer: a) −a/b

Neutral temperature is where emf is maximum (dE/dθ = 0).

E = aθ + ½bθ²
dE/dθ = a + bθ = 0
θn = −a/b
10 The value of magnetizing field (H) when the magnetic flux density (B) is equal to zero, is called
a) Retentivity
b) Coercivity
c) Curie's temperature
d) Neutral temperature
Answer: b) Coercivity

Coercivity is the reverse magnetizing field H required to reduce B to zero after magnetization. Retentivity is the value of B when H = 0.

11 What is the point where the seismic waves start, called?
a) Epicenter
b) Hypocenter
c) Metacenter
d) Seismic center
Answer: b) Hypocenter

The hypocenter (or focus) is the point within the Earth where an earthquake rupture starts. The epicenter is the point directly above it on the Earth's surface.

12 a) Define torque. Derive a relation between torque and moment of inertia. [2]
Solution:

Torque: The turning effect of a force about an axis: τ = F × r.

Derivation: For a particle of mass mi at distance ri:

Fi = miriα
τi = Firi = miri²α
Total τ = Σmiri²α = Iα
τ = Iα
b) Calculate the radius of gyration of a uniform rod of mass 20 kg and length 30 cm about an axis passing through one end and perpendicular to its length. [1]
Solution:
I = ML²/3 = 20 × (0.30)²/3 = 0.6 kg m²
K = √(I/M) = √(0.6/20) = √0.03
K = 0.173 m = 17.3 cm
c) State and explain the principle of conservation of angular momentum. [2]
Solution:

Principle: If no external torque acts, total angular momentum remains constant: Iω = constant.

When a spinning person pulls their arms in, I decreases and ω increases proportionally. This explains divers tucking to spin faster.

12 (OR) a) Explain why a water drop sticks on glass but mercury does not. [1]
Solution:

Water: Adhesive force (water-glass) > Cohesive force. Water spreads and sticks (wetting).

Mercury: Cohesive force > Adhesive force. Mercury forms droplets and does not stick (non-wetting).

Solution:
Mass flow rate is constant:
ρA1v1 = ρA2v2

For incompressible fluid:
A1v1 = A2v2 = constant

Where the tube is narrow, fluid flows faster.

14 a) State first law of thermodynamics and derive Mayer's relation Cp − Cv = R. [3]
Solution:

First Law: dQ = dU + dW = dU + PdV

At constant volume: dQ = CvdT = dU

At constant pressure: dQ = CpdT = dU + PdV = CvdT + RdT

Group C: Long Answer Questions [3 × 8 = 24]
20 a) What is interference? Discuss the importance of coherent sources. [2]
Solution:

Interference: The superposition of two waves resulting in a pattern of alternating bright and dark regions (constructive and destructive interference).

Importance of coherent sources: Coherent sources maintain a constant phase difference, producing stable and observable interference patterns. Without coherence, the phase difference changes rapidly and the pattern averages out to uniform illumination.

b) Show that width of bright fringe equals width of dark fringe in Young's double slit experiment. [3]
Solution:

Position of nth bright fringe: ynbright = nλD/d

Position of nth dark fringe: yndark = (2n−1)λD/(2d)

Fringe width (bright) = yn+1b − ynb = λD/d
Fringe width (dark) = yn+1d − ynd = λD/d

Both fringe widths are equal: β = λD/d
c) Why is a diffraction grating better than double slit for measuring wavelength? [1]
Solution:

A diffraction grating produces sharper, brighter, and widely separated maxima. The angular separation is large and measurable accurately, whereas double-slit fringes are broad and less distinct.

d) Unpolarized light incident at 55° on glass. Reflected beam is completely polarized. Find refractive index and angle of refraction. [2]
Solution:

By Brewster's law: μ = tan(ip)

μ = tan(55°) = 1.428

Also, μ = sin(ip)/sin(r)
sin(r) = sin(55°)/1.428 = 0.819/1.428 = 0.574
r = 35.0°

(Note: ip + r = 90° at Brewster angle, so r = 35°)

21 a) Define RMS value of alternating current. [1]
Solution:

The RMS (Root Mean Square) value of AC is the steady DC current that produces the same heating effect in a given resistor in a given time as the AC does. For sinusoidal current: Irms = I0/√2.

b) Discuss LCR circuit with phasor diagram to find current and impedance. [2]
Solution:

In an LCR series circuit, voltages across L and C are 180° out of phase. The net reactive voltage is VL − VC.

V = √[VR² + (VL − VC)²]
Impedance: Z = √[R² + (XL − XC)²]
where XL = ωL, XC = 1/(ωC)

Phase angle: tanφ = (XL − XC)/R
Current: I = V/Z

Phasor diagram: VR along x-axis, VL upward, VC downward. Resultant V is the vector sum.

c) What is electrical resonance? Obtain expression for resonant frequency. Draw current vs frequency curve. [3]
Solution:

Electrical resonance: The condition in an LCR circuit when XL = XC, making the circuit purely resistive and current maximum.

XL = XC
ω0L = 1/(ω0C)
ω0² = 1/(LC)
f0 = 1/(2π√LC)

Current vs frequency curve: Bell-shaped curve peaking at f0. Sharp peak for low resistance (high Q-factor), broad peak for high resistance.

d) LCR circuit: R=25Ω, L=30 mH, C=10 µF, Erms=220 V, f=50 Hz. Find current. [2]
Solution:
XL = 2πfL = 2π × 50 × 0.030 = 9.42 Ω
XC = 1/(2πfC) = 1/(2π × 50 × 10×10−6) = 318.3 Ω
Z = √[25² + (9.42 − 318.3)²]
Z = √[625 + 95308] = √95933 = 309.7 Ω

I = V/Z = 220/309.7 = 0.71 A

21 (OR) a) State Lenz's law and explain demonstration with coil and magnet. [2]
Solution:

Lenz's Law: The induced current always flows in a direction that opposes the change causing it.

Demonstration: When a bar magnet's N-pole is pushed toward a coil, induced current creates a N-pole on the near side of the coil, repelling the magnet. When pulled away, it creates a S-pole, attracting the magnet. This opposition requires work, converting mechanical energy to electrical energy.

b) Vehicle brake with aluminium disc and electromagnets. [3]
Solution:

i) How emf induction causes slowing: The rotating aluminium disc cuts through the magnetic field of the electromagnets, inducing eddy currents in the disc (Faraday's law). By Lenz's law, these currents create a magnetic field that opposes the motion of the disc, producing a braking torque that slows the vehicle.

ii) Why braking increases with speed: Faster rotation means the disc cuts more magnetic flux per second (greater dΦ/dt), inducing larger eddy currents. Since braking force is proportional to eddy current, higher speed produces stronger braking.

c) Find emf in a conductor: l=20 cm, r=10 cm from axis, B=0.5 T, 1000 rpm. [3]
Solution:
ω = 2πN/60 = 2π × 1000/60 = 104.7 rad/s
v = rω = 0.10 × 104.7 = 10.47 m/s

emf = Blv = 0.5 × 0.20 × 10.47
emf = 1.047 V ≈ 1.05 V
22 Carbon-14 has half-life 5760 years. Produced by: 147N + 10n → 146C + X
a) Define half-life and decay constant. Relation between them. [2]
Solution:

Half-life (T1/2): Time for half the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay.

Decay constant (λ): Probability of decay per unit time.

T1/2 = ln(2)/λ = 0.693/λ
b) Identify particle X. [1]
Solution:

Balancing mass and atomic numbers: X is a proton (11H or 11p).

c) What is radio-carbon dating? How to estimate age of ancient objects? [3]
Solution:

Radio-carbon dating: A method to determine the age of carbon-containing archaeological or geological samples by measuring the remaining C-14 activity.

Living organisms maintain equilibrium C-14 levels. After death, C-14 decays without replenishment. By comparing the activity of a sample to that of living matter, the age is calculated using:

N = N0e−λt
t = (1/λ) ln(N0/N) = (T1/2/0.693) ln(N0/N)
d) Sample contains 1 × 1022 C-14 nuclei. What is the activity? [2]
Solution:
T1/2 = 5760 years = 5760 × 365.25 × 24 × 3600 s
T1/2 = 1.817 × 1011 s
λ = 0.693/T1/2 = 3.81 × 10−12 s−1

Activity A = λN
A = 3.81 × 10−12 × 1022
A = 3.81 × 1010 Bq
= 3.81 × 1010 / 3.7 × 1010 Ci
A ≈ 1.03 Ci

22 (OR) a) Modern method of X-ray production.
i) Working principle. [1]
Solution:

High-speed electrons are accelerated by high voltage and strike a metal target. Their sudden deceleration produces X-rays (bremsstrahlung), and characteristic X-rays are produced when electrons knock out inner-shell electrons.

ii) Why metal target of high melting point and high atomic weight? [2]
Solution:

High melting point: Only a small fraction of electron energy becomes X-rays; most becomes heat. High melting point prevents the target from melting (e.g., tungsten: 3422°C).

High atomic weight: Heavier atoms have more protons, creating stronger nuclear fields for electron deceleration, producing more intense X-rays with higher energy.

iii) How are intensity and quality of X-rays controlled? [2]
Solution:

Intensity: Controlled by filament current (heater current). More current means more thermionic emission, more electrons, more X-rays.

Quality (penetrating power): Controlled by accelerating voltage (kV). Higher voltage gives electrons more energy, producing shorter wavelength, more penetrating X-rays.

b) Light λ=400 nm on photosensitive material with work function 2.3 eV. Find maximum KE of photoelectrons. [3]
Solution:
E = hc/λ = (6.63×10−34 × 3×108)/(400×10−9)
E = 4.97×10−19 J = 3.10 eV

KEmax = E − φ = 3.10 − 2.30
KEmax = 0.80 eV
= 0.80 × 1.6×10−19 J
= 1.28 × 10−19 J
